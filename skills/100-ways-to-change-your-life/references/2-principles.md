## Key Principles

### 1. Wellness Is a Tool, Not a Destination
"The second that wellness is making your life worse, not better, it's no longer wellness." If you're skipping dinners with friends because of your diet, that's not health — that's suffering. Wellness exists in service of your life, not the other way around.

### 2. N-of-1 Over Population Studies
Randomized controlled trials tell us what works for populations. N-of-1 experiments tell us what works for YOU. Before adding a supplement, changing a diet, or starting a routine: define baseline, change one variable, measure the result. This prevents wasted effort on things that don't serve you.

### 3. Start with Your Why
"Most people over-index on the how and under-index on the why." (Daniel Pink) Before asking "How do I stick to a workout?" ask "Why do I want to exercise?" The why unlocks intrinsic motivation that willpower alone can't sustain.

> **Case: Daniel Pink on why vs how** (Tip 4): Pink explains that shifting from "How am I going to exercise?" to "Why am I exercising?" reveals deeper motives — mental clarity, family presence, longevity. These intrinsic reasons sustain motivation longer than any external reward or punishment. **Key takeaway:** Spend 5 minutes on why before you spend 5 hours on how. Purpose beats discipline.

### 4. Start Impossibly Small
"When I was lying in bed, unable to leave the house, I committed to one thing: a green smoothie every morning." Not a full morning routine. Not a diet overhaul. One action. From that single habit, everything else grew. The smallest possible unit of change is the only unit that can create permanent change.

### 5. Never Pre-Reject Yourself
The most common reason people don't achieve things is that they reject themselves before anyone else can. Don't say "they won't hire me" — apply. Don't say "they won't respond" — email. The world will reject you enough. Don't do their job for them.

### 6. Your Brain Likes Immediate Gratification
This is not a character flaw — it's neuroscience. If your habit doesn't provide an immediate reward, you won't stick to it. The solution: either find the immediate reward (how does this feel RIGHT NOW?) or pair the habit with immediate pleasure (temptation bundling — listen to your favorite podcast only while exercising).

### 7. Suffering Less Is the Ultimate Metric
"Suffering less is my overarching philosophy." Not more productivity. Not more discipline. Not more health. Less suffering. If something increases your total suffering — even if it's "healthy" — it's not serving you. Trust the metric.

> **Case: The workout that stopped being wellness** (Tip 1): Moody describes a woman who forced herself through hour-long HIIT classes she hated. She was "fit" but miserable. When she switched to walking with a friend — something she enjoyed — she moved less intensely but more consistently, and her overall health improved more. **Key takeaway:** Enjoyable movement beats perfect movement. Consistency trumps intensity. Suffering less IS the point.
