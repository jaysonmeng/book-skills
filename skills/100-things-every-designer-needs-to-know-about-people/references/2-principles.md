## Key Principles

### 1. Perception Is Construction
What people see is not reality — it's their brain's best guess based on past experience, expectations, and incomplete sensory data. This means two users may see the same screen differently. Design assumptions must be validated with real users.

### 2. Recognition Is Easier Than Recall
The brain is optimized for recognition (matching current input to stored patterns), not recall (rebuilding a pattern from scratch). Show users what they need instead of making them remember it. This is why search works better than navigation trees.

### 3. Chunk Information into 4s
Short-term memory holds exactly 4 items. This applies to navigation items, form fields, product features, and options. More than 4 requires chunking. There is no 7±2 — that's been disproven.

### 4. Variable Rewards Drive Engagement
Dopamine is released not by receiving a reward, but by ANTICIPATING the reward — especially when the reward is unpredictable. This is the neuroscience behind slot machines, Twitter, and email checking. Use this power deliberately and ethically.

> **Case: The 10-minute attention limit** (Thing 44): Research shows that sustained attention to any one stimulus lasts about 10 minutes. After that, the brain needs a break or a shift. TED Talks are 18 minutes — but they're designed with emotional peaks every 3-4 minutes to reset attention. **Key takeaway:** Design content and tasks to fit within 10-minute blocks. If longer, insert natural break points or emotional resets every 3-4 minutes.

### 5. Intrinsic Motivation Beats Extrinsic
People are motivated by autonomy, mastery, purpose, and progress. External rewards (money, points, badges) actually DECREASE intrinsic motivation for interesting tasks. Use extrinsic rewards sparingly — they work best for boring tasks you want people to start.

### 6. Emotion Is the Decision Engine
Patients with damage to the emotional brain cannot make decisions, even though their logic is intact. If your product doesn't make people feel anything, they won't act. Emotional responses happen faster than conscious thought. Design for feeling first, function second.

### 7. Errors Are Inevitable — Design for Recovery
"People will always make mistakes. There is no fail-safe product." (Thing 85). The best design approach is not to prevent every possible error — it's to make errors easy to detect and recover from. Provide clear feedback, undo options, and forgiveness.

> **Case: The unconscious decides first** (Thing 90): fMRI studies show that the brain makes decisions up to 7 seconds before the conscious mind is aware of them. The conscious mind then creates a post-hoc rationalization. **Key takeaway:** The unconscious brain has already decided before the user "thinks" about it. Design the unconscious path — visual appeal, emotional resonance, first impressions. The conscious reasons will follow to justify the choice.
