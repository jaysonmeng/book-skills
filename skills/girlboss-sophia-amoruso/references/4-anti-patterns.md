# Anti-Patterns: Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

## Anti-Pattern 1: Waiting for the Perfect Plan

**The mistake:** Waiting until you have a business plan, funding, and certainty before starting. Amoruso started with $50 and no plan.

**The correction:** Start before you are ready. The perfect plan does not exist. Your first idea will evolve. What matters is starting.

> **Case: The paralysis of analysis** (Chapter 2): Amoruso describes friends who would talk about starting a business for years without ever doing it. "They had spreadsheets, business plans, pitch decks. I had a box of vintage clothes and an eBay account. Five years later, I had a company. They still had spreadsheets."

## Anti-Pattern 2: Spending Money You Don't Have

**The mistake:** Raising money before you have revenue and spending it on unnecessary things (office, furniture, salaries).

**The correction:** Bootstrap. Spend only what you earn. If you can't afford something, find a way to do without it or do it yourself.

> **Case: The competitor who raised too much** (Chapter 8): "A competitor raised $5 million to compete with us. They built a beautiful office, hired 50 people, and launched a huge marketing campaign. Within 18 months, they were gone. They burned through all their capital without building a sustainable business. We were still in our studio apartment, still profitable."

## Anti-Pattern 3: Ignoring the Details

**The mistake:** Thinking that grand strategy matters more than execution. In reality, the small things — the shipping label, the packaging, the customer service response time — define your brand.

**The correction:** Care about every detail. If it touches the customer, it matters. Your brand is the sum of all the small things you do.

> **Case: The handwritten note** (Chapter 9): Nasty Gal included a handwritten thank-you note with every order. It was inefficient. It didn't scale. But customers loved it. "People would post photos of their purchase on social media, and the handwritten note was often the first thing they mentioned. That note was more valuable than any ad we could have bought."

## Anti-Pattern 4: Playing Small

**The mistake:** Underpricing your product because you're afraid of being rejected. Not asking for what you deserve.

**The correction:** Price based on value, not cost. Ask for what you're worth. The worst that happens is a no, and a no is not fatal.

> **Case: The vintage dealer who priced too low** (Chapter 3): "I saw a vintage seller list a beautiful 1950s dress for $20. She was afraid to ask for more because she thought no one would pay it. I bought it from her and resold it for $150. The dress was worth $150. She just didn't believe it. Don't be that seller."

## Anti-Pattern 5: Trying to Please Everyone

**The mistake:** Building a product or brand that tries to appeal to everyone. You end up appealing to no one.

**The correction:** Niche down. Know exactly who your customer is and serve them obsessively. When you try to please everyone, you water down your brand.
